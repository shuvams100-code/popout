## The blocker — read this part

**I cannot currently build the "Tonight in Bangalore" pipeline, and that's the format the whole
strategy rests on.**

Every aggregator that holds this data blocks automated reading: allevents.in and insider.in
refuse the fetch outright, HighApe serves only navigation on its listing pages, and most real
recurring events in this city live on Instagram — the one surface I can't read systematically.

I'm not going to route around that by scraping sites that forbid it. That's how you get the
domain blocked and the account banned, and it's a bad trade for a product whose only asset is
trust.

**Three routes that are legitimate, in order of how much I like them:**

1. **Browser automation.** This session has TinyFish and Claude-in-Chrome tools that read pages
   the way a person does. This is the intended tool for exactly this job and the one I'd try first.
2. **Open APIs.** Meetup and Eventbrite both have real APIs and both have genuine Bangalore
   activity — particularly for the tech-adjacent, new-in-town crowd who are precisely your ICP.
3. **Curated recurring series.** What's in this document. Slow to build, but it's the most
   durable: a weekly night doesn't expire, and thirty of them generates a full calendar forever
   with no scraping at all. This is the one that survives every other route breaking.

**My recommendation:** 3 as the foundation, 1 to fill the daily slots, 2 as a supplement.

Until one of those is running, posts 1, 3, 4, 5 and 7 can ship on verified data. Posts 2 and 6
cannot. Five posts a week beats seven with two of them fabricated.
